# World journeys, version 1

Initial editorial selections, 24 September 2026. These short visits use existing canonical lessons for grades 1–4. They are not complete subject curricula, a prerequisite waiver, or an exhaustive pedagogical review. Default term routes remain separate. No lesson bodies, progress identifiers or family plans are changed by this manifest.

## Implemented browsing stage

- Parent atlas entry and the sample-map overlay open grade journeys. The grade stays in the URL and remains selected during the mounted browsing session. Original collections remain reachable, including existing Farm/Fair term choices and neighbouring-world navigation.
- Old world and lesson URLs still open their original collections. Grade changes from a shared overlay link replace that overlay history entry; Close closes the overlay.
- The child can choose Grade journey inside an already accessible world, then return to My lessons. Current-day entry and atlas permissions are unchanged. Only existing today's sheets are playable; finished canonical lessons use their saved answers. Future members show closed cards and make no lesson-body or state requests.
- The manifest resolves 144 nonempty world/grade visits across 36 worlds, using 172 unique existing lessons in 297 memberships. The full 336-lesson library remains accessible through the original routes. The two Fossil Cliffs foundation visits are thin, not complete geology sequences.
- Rendering uses the existing bounded paper preparation and scene residency. Membership is lightweight metadata. Visiting a journey does not synthesize a learning year, change prerequisites, emit progress, or award a world's annual completion moment.
- Atlas browsing labels describe actual available grade memberships. Scheduled child geography, annual labels, family choices and rewards remain the original model. No database migration or family-setting write is introduced.

This is the browsing and reuse stage, not completion of the entire curriculum roadmap. The following are still outstanding:

1. Old Tower needs authored and reviewed history sequences. Ferry Town needs a chosen target language, teaching sequence and appropriate audio/assessment support. They remain explicit gaps.
2. Preschool and grade 5 remain outside the current pack; do not advertise empty choices as supported grades.
3. The selections below need full pedagogical/body review before they are promoted from optional themed visits into assigned curriculum. Reading all bodies, checking prerequisites and authoring identified gaps are content work, not a renderer capability.
4. Arbitrary assigned lessons in a parent-selected setting, per-subject grade contexts, editable family journeys and journey-specific milestones are not shipped here. They need explicit persistence and progress contracts. Current plans and rewards are intentionally unchanged.
5. Large chapter indexing and durable return anchors beyond the current mounted visit remain future work. These initial visits contain one to four lessons and reuse the bounded renderer.
6. Physical iPhone crash validation remains separate; desktop and phone-WebKit checks cannot establish device memory safety.

## Selection and scope

Each cell is an explicit ordered sequence. Themes favour the world's setting and a coherent small learning activity; they do not fill a quota or clone the full library. `curated` means two or more selected lessons, `thin` means one, and `gap` means no published selection. Status measures availability, not curriculum quality or mastery. The runtime rechecks actual pack membership and grade; unavailable IDs are reported and omitted, never silently replaced. A changed membership requires a new journey version and explicit migration policy for any persisted visit identity.

Canonical prerequisites outside a visit still apply. The reader must consult the canonical learning plan and must not construct a synthetic year, rewrite requirements, award full-world completion from a filtered subset, or replay rewards for a reused lesson. A repeated lesson is the same lesson everywhere. These sequences deliberately overlap where a lesson fits several places; they are optional routes through shared material, not extra assignments.

## Context limitations and gaps

- The old tower needs authored, sourced history lessons. Ferry town needs a target language decision and authored language lessons. Both have four honest gap cells; English literacy is not substituted for foreign-language teaching.
- The open sea and coral reef begin with familiar **pond** lessons in grade 1. Their content remains explicitly freshwater; this is a foundation for discussing aquatic homes, not a claim that pond animals live on a reef.
- Fossil cliffs has direct fossil formation only in grade 2. Grade 1 develops close observation and material language. Grade 3 has one filtering lesson as a sediment-separation foundation; grade 4 has one weathering/erosion lesson. These are intentionally thin geology visits, not invented fossil units.
- Grade 3 observatory and night-sky visits use artistic observation of light plus measured weather. They do not teach astronomy at that grade. Grade 2 uses shadows; grades 1 and 4 have direct sun/moon content.
- Grade 4 cloud islands links air resistance with a rain artwork; this is a cross-subject weather visit, not a meteorology course. Winter fair is a year-round money/pattern/message visit; no seasonal lesson wording is rewritten.
- Music visits require the instruments already stated in their lessons. Coding visits retain their original environments. Parent choice of a world does not remove prerequisites, required materials, accessibility needs or safety instructions in the canonical lessons.
- Read all lesson bodies before calling this fully reviewed curriculum. Titles were checked across the pack; goals and full bodies were checked for the contextual lessons noted above. No new history/language content has been generated.
