# Story

Status: proposed, and built into the journal, September 2026. This is the series bible for the journal's worlds: the premise, the cast, each year's arc, each world's chapter and the moment in it, what a child's work makes happen on the map and what earns each thing, and the refusals the design keeps. It is written against [journal.md](journal.md), which describes the roll, the worlds and the map as built, and against the limits in [product.md](product.md) and [ai.md](ai.md), which this document does not relax. The worlds' chapters are data in `school/worlds/`, the rewards are computed in `school/worlds/rewards.ts`, and `test/world.test.ts` in the scratchpad and `school/worlds/__tests__/worlds.test.ts` hold every world to the rules below.

## The premise

The journal is a map that is drawn as the child goes. It starts at a garden gate in a meadow in the first term of the first year and ends at a lantern at the top of an island in the last term of the fourth, and the island can be seen from the harbour in the first year, small, on the edge of the sky, with smoke coming off it. Between the two there are twelve worlds, three a year, each a place the child lives in for a term, joined by the way the story travels there: a path, a road, rails, a river, and last of all the sea. A year is a story with a beginning, a middle and an end, and a world is a chapter of it: something is waiting to happen in each one from the day the child arrives, it happens on the day the last lesson there is finished in the tracks the family is doing, and from somewhere along the way the next part of the map can be seen before it is reached.

Round the twelve stand eleven more, built as worlds in the journal and set out in [overworld.md](overworld.md): the garden, the child's own, on the other side of the meadow's gate in the year before the first; a far shore of three worlds across the water from the island, for a fifth year; a winter fair and a farm, which a family can choose for a term instead of the term's own world; and five places the science, music, history, language and art lessons bring a child to, one on each year's land for every year that holds their lessons. They follow every rule below, and none of them changes the road from the garden gate to the lantern.

The child is the one travelling. Nothing in the story asks the child to be anyone, to feel anything or to come back for any reason other than the next lesson, and everything that happens on the map happens because of work the child has done.

## The guide, and what the guide may not be

The brief for this work asked that the guide have somewhere to get to and a reason to go. [ai.md](ai.md) rules out a guide with a name, a backstory, feelings, preferences, memory in its own voice or affection, and says that nothing should invite a child to form a relationship with the guide. We kept ai.md, and the two fit once the destination and the reason are given to the map rather than to the guide.

The destination is the lantern at the top of the island, and the reason to go is visible rather than told: the path leads there, the island is on the horizon from the first year, and each world's moment is sketched in pencil at its foot until the work inks it. The guide walks the path because the child does. It keeps the repertoire ai.md allows, which is a pose, a direction to look and a line an author wrote, and its lines on the journal stay in the second person or the imperative and about the place: "This is the night sky. Look up." It never says "I", never greets the child by name, never remarks on an absence and never celebrates a streak, because there is nothing for it to celebrate. The seven guide designs recur by world rather than following the child, so no single guide becomes a companion across four years; the firefly is simply the light that lives in the first world, the night hill and the island.

## The cast

The recurring characters are creatures and places. Neither has any lines, so neither can become a persona, and both can grow up in the only way a drawing can: by what it is doing and how many of it there are.

- The rabbit is one rabbit in the meadow, two in the woods a year later, awake past bedtime on the night hill, and a pair of hares in the snow in the mountains.
- The fox is by the railway embankment in the first year, in the woods in the second, on the night hill in the third and in the mountains in the fourth.
- The owl is in the woods and on the night hill, and it turns up as the island's secret at the very end.
- The gull stands on the harbour's boardwalk in the first year and flies alongside the ship in the fourth. The crab is one crab at the harbour and a family of three on the island.
- The lighthouse is the harbour's gate and its moment in the first year, and in the fourth it goes by very small on the horizon of the open sea.
- The island is on the harbour's horizon in the first year and the sea's in the fourth, and it is the last world.
- The mountains are on the night hill's horizon, under the stars, a year before the child climbs them.

## Each year's arc

The first year leaves home. It starts in a meadow with a footpath through the grass, comes down to the sea at the harbour, where the child first sees the island, and ends at the railway with a train that waits on the horizon all term and pulls in on the last day.

The second year is the seasons. It arrives in the woods at dusk, under the palette's purple and gold sky, with stepping stones through the leaves and a moon that is full by the end; it spends the winter indoors in a warm kitchen with snow at the window and a cake in the oven; and it goes out into the town in spring, where the clock tower strikes twelve at the end.

The third year is looking further. It begins on a hill at night under a real dark blue sky, with a rocket on its pad and the mountains of the next year on the horizon; it spends the winter on the sports ground, where the podium's third step fills; and it ends in the laboratory, where the bulb on the bench lights.

The fourth year is the adventure the others have been pointing at. The child climbs the mountains, where it goes below zero and the top is always in view, and from the top the sea can be seen. The open sea is a winter crossing in a storm, with icebergs and a whale, the harbour's lighthouse going by, and the island getting nearer on the horizon. The last world is the island itself, reached by boat, under an orange evening sky, with a line of lanterns up to the one at the top.

## Each world's chapter

Each world's moment is drawn in pencil at the foot of its stretch of the roll from the first day and inked on the day the last lesson of its term in the tracks the family's plan has on is finished, with its line under it. Where the moment is something the world's gate does, the gate waits in its before state all term: the harbour's lighthouse is dark and the sports ground's podium has an empty step. Each world's secret is tucked away at the far edge of the world beside its third day. The glimpse is a later world standing small on this world's horizon.

The last six were drawn first as concept scenes ([worlds-next.md](worlds-next.md)) and are now places a track's lessons bring a child to, like the marsh: each holds real lessons by their ids, stands at its own site off the run on the land of each year whose lessons it holds, and is stamped there once one of that year's lessons is finished. None of them gates a lesson, and none keeps a count of what was found.

The last nine are the places further off, built so that every lesson outside maths has a place of its own. Six stand on isles in the water round each land whose year holds their lessons, and three stand by the sea on those lands. Its way leaves from a world of its own year; where that world is past what the child has reached, the colour stops short of it, so its land stays pencil and only the way is drawn. Each isle is coloured to its coasts once its moment has happened.

## What a child's work makes happen

Everything below is worked out from the record each time the journal or the map is drawn, in `school/worlds/rewards.ts`, and nothing is stored beside it. The record is the fold of the log that the map and the parent's week already read: a lesson is finished when a screen sitting ends finished (`sitting-ended` with `finished: true`) or when a printed sheet's questions are marked (`marked`), and each finished lesson carries the day it was finished. A grown-up sees every mark on the same map as the child, and in the grown-up view each world's note says which lesson and which day made each mark.

| What happens | What earns it | What it reads | Where the child sees it | Where a grown-up sees it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A road is inked | The first lesson finished in the world it leads to, or the last lesson finished in the world it leaves | The finished lessons and their days, by term | On the map, the way into the next world goes from a pencil sketch to its own kind: a path, a road, rails, a river or a sea lane | The same road, and the stamp at its far end with the day |
| A stamp in a world | The first lesson finished there | The first finished lesson of the term | A round stamp on the world's picture with its name and the day | The same stamp; the note names the lesson |
| A landmark lit | The lesson it belongs beside, finished | The finished lesson whose drawings, skills or track the landmark's reach names | On the roll, the landmark beside that day glows, and the one beside today waits for today's work; on the place the world is seen as, it stands lit beside that day's stop | The note lists each lit landmark with the lesson and the day |
| A day stamped | The day's own lessons finished | The finished lessons of that day | On the place, the day's stop carries a stamp with the world's own creature in the ring, a gull at the harbour and a hen at the meadow | The same stamp, beside the day the note already names |
| A creature walks with the guide | The lesson it belongs beside, finished | As for a landmark, for the world's creatures | Up to three walk behind the guide beside today's sheet, behind the guide at today's stop on the place, and behind the guide on the map | The note says which creature walks with the guide and from which day |
| The world's moment | The last lesson of the world's term in the tracks the plan has on, finished | Every lesson of the term in those tracks finished, and the day of the last | The pencil sketch at the foot of the world is inked with its line, which on the place stands at the end of the trail, where the child can see it waiting from their first day; where it is the gate's moment, the gate changes; on the map the world's picture shows the moment | The note gives the day, and before it the sketch says what inks it |
| A secret | Nothing but the day it sits beside being finished | Whether that day is behind the child | At the far edge of the world beside its third day, off the path; when the camera comes to rest with it near the middle of the screen it moves a little and its line is said aloud | Its place is ringed, with the day it has been there since |
| A lantern lit | The world's moment | The day of the world's moment | On the map, the lantern by the road into the world is lit; a finished year is three lights along its road, and after four years the road is lit all the way to the island's lantern, which is the last of them | The same lanterns, the whole string in view; the world's note gives the moment's day |
| A land coloured to its coasts | Every world on that land finished | The same | On the map the colour reaches every coast of the land, and the islands off it are coloured in with it | The same, and the year's name is underlined with the day the year was finished |
| The jetty inked, and the sail | Every world of the year finished inks the land's jetty; the next year's first lesson opens the next land | The same, and the day of the next year's first lesson | The jetty, the ship at it and the path down to it go from pencil to ink; on the day the next land opens, the child's map is that land and the ship comes in to it, and under reduced motion it simply opens there | Not yet: a grown-up's map does not draw the jetties |
| A place becomes available | The previous world's way opens | The first day of the next world | Every world and subject place is visible in pencil, the next is set apart, and unavailable places cannot be entered | The whole country, in pencil ahead |

A world's moment waits only for the tracks the family's plan has on. The plan is read from the log the way the parent's pages read it, from the `track` changes of `plan-changed`, and a plan that has never named a track has every track on, which is how the sample child is shown. The rule used to wait for every lesson of every track in the term, 13 to 30 lessons, so a family doing only maths never saw a moment, a lit lantern or a land coloured to its coasts. The plan is read as it stood on each day: a moment happens on the first day every lesson of the term in the tracks then on is finished, so turning a track on after a moment has happened leaves the moment where it is, and a track that was already on when the term was worked makes the moment wait for its lessons too. A stamp, a lit landmark and a creature walking with the guide are earned by a lesson finished in any track, and a later change to the plan does not move them.

A secret is found by looking at it and is not written down. We considered a `found` event, so that a found secret would stay found and a grown-up could see which had been found. We did not take it, for two reasons. Recording where a child looked is not something the product needs to teach, and [product.md](product.md) says we collect what the product needs to teach and nothing else. And a record of found secrets is one step from a count of them, which is the collection this design refuses. A secret found once is found again the next time, the way a picture in a book is.

A term with work in it keeps its world in the grown-up's panel. The marks above belong to the work, but a lit landmark and a moment are drawn in the world the work was done in, so changing that world afterwards would take them away; the panel says why the choice is fixed.

## The story at the scale of the whole map

The map of every world is drawn as an illustrated map of a country of lands and seas, set out in [overworld.md](overworld.md), and it makes the premise literal: a child's map is the land of their year, with the sea round it, where every world and subject place of that year is visible, unavailable places are dimmed and cannot be entered, and the child's work colours in what they have walked, so the map is visibly the child's own, and its title says whose it is and the day it was begun. It adds one thread that runs through all four years to the lantern at the top of the island: every world has a lantern by the road into it, lit on the day its moment happens, so the island's lantern is the last of twelve rather than a surprise; a child sees the ones they have lit on their land, and from the harbour in the first year they see the island's smoke on the harbour's own horizon. It counts nothing a child can see as a number and can never go out, for the same reason a moment cannot. The guide rides what each way is, a train along a land's rails, a boat down its river, a ship across the sea from one year's land to the next, which is how a year ends, the hot-air balloon up to the cloud islands, and the child can fly the paper plane between the worlds they have reached, with nothing to win or lose on the way. The cast stands in its places on the map when the camera is close, the worlds' secrets are drawn only close in so they are found by looking, and once in a visit a hot-air balloon drifts across the child's land, never announced or kept.

## The refusals, kept

These are [product.md](product.md)'s refusals as they apply to the map, and each is kept by construction rather than by care.

- No streaks. Nothing counts days in a row, nothing notices a day missed, and the map has no calendar on it: a world's moment happens when its lessons are done, whenever that is.
- No timers used as pressure. Nothing on the journal or the map is timed, and nothing expires.
- No hidden adaptive algorithm. Every mark is one of the rules in the table above, each a plain function of lessons finished, and a grown-up sees the lesson and the day behind every mark.
- No currency. Nothing is counted up to be spent, and no total of stamps, lit landmarks or secrets appears anywhere on the child's surface. The only count on the map is in the grown-up's note, and it is the plan's own: lessons finished of the term's lessons in the tracks the plan has on.
- Nothing bought, and nothing that could be.
- Nothing a child has earned taken away. A mark is a function of finished lessons, and a finished lesson stays finished, so a mark once made stays made; the test runs every grade from a few weeks in to late in the year and checks that more work only ever adds to the map. The one way a mark could move, a grown-up changing the world of a term with work in it, is closed, and a moment is read against the plan as it stood when it happened, so turning a track on afterwards does not undo it.
- Nothing gates a lesson. The map follows the lessons rather than leading them: a child travels between the worlds they have reached and to the one their learning has just opened, while every other world remains visible but unavailable. Every lesson of the year is on the roll, and opening or locking a lesson remains the plan's job.
- No guide persona, as above.

## What this needs from the data model

Nothing new. Every mark reads the finished lessons and their days, which the fold of `sitting-ended` and `marked` already produces for the parent's week, and the rules are code a parent's page can state in a sentence each. The one event this design could have added, for secrets, is rejected above. The world choice is that event, `world-chosen`, read as it stood when each term's first work happened, so a term with work in it keeps its world and its world's tweaks ([api.md](api.md), "A family").

## Open questions

- Whether a world's moment should play once, the first time the child opens the journal after it happened, rather than being found already inked. The engine's timeline can stage it and collapse it for reduced motion, and a cue could carry a sound from [sound.md](sound.md), which this work does not own.
- What the map does after the fourth year. The island is the end of the map as drawn; a fifth grade would need the map to go on past it, and the lantern would stop being the end. The far shore's three worlds are now built (above), so what is missing is a fifth year of lessons and the second sheet of the map. [overworld.md](overworld.md) proposes an answer, and the country already holds it: from the lantern at the top of the island a far shore is across the northern ocean, and a fifth year is a new sheet of paper taped along the top of the first, so nothing already drawn is drawn again.
- Whether a grown-up should be able to see which secrets a child has found. We think not, for the reasons above, and it needs the owner's decision because it is the one place the design declines to record something a parent might want.
- Whether the moments should take the lessons' own numbers, as the landmarks could: the clock tower striking the hour of the term's last clock lesson, the podium's steps showing the scores the lesson asked about.
