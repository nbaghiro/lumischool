# The next worlds

Status: the six ranked first are built, as places a track's lessons bring a child to, September 2026; five more became worlds in a second round, as places further off (see "Candidates that became worlds"); the rest are proposed. This document answers the owner's request to extend the map with more worlds, each with its own style, concept and art. It sets out nineteen candidate worlds beyond the twenty-three there are or will be, with what makes each one its own place, how we ranked them, the six we would build first and the concept scene drawn for each, what each would teach and to which years, where each would stand on the map and how a child would get there, the drawings added for them, and what building each as a full journal world would take. The six are worlds on the Worlds tab, each one on its own (`world.html?place=coral-reef`) and all six after the worlds to come; the concept scenes they were drawn from are gone, since the worlds replaced them. It is written against [journal.md](journal.md) for what a world is, [story.md](story.md) for the chapter and the refusals, [overworld.md](overworld.md) for the map and how it grows, [motion.md](motion.md) and [animation.md](animation.md) for what may move, and [art.md](art.md) for the shelf, and it relaxes none of them.

## What the owner asked for

The owner's words, after seeing the new map: "i love this map so much, lets start a subtask to figure out how to extend this beauty with more worlds with each one coming with its own unique style, concept, arts, and always with the utmost bar for visual appealingness and beauty". The brief that followed asked for at least fifteen candidates, each tied to what it would teach and placed on the map, a ranking, concept scenes for the top six drawn from the shelf and new shelf drawings, and proposals for the map itself, without building any of them as a journal world yet, so that the owner can choose which become real after seeing them.

## What a new world has to be

A world, as [journal.md](journal.md) sets it out, has its own ground, path and horizon, its own light, weather and seasons, landmarks, creatures and a guide, a moment the term waits for, a secret, a rare sight and the marks a child's work makes on it. Since the worlds work added the eleven, the world test holds every world to a ground, a path, a light, a gate, a moment and a rare sight of its own, so a new world cannot be an old one in another colour. Twenty-three worlds already take the obvious places: the meadow, the harbour, the railway, the woods, the kitchen, the town, the night sky, the sports ground, the laboratory, the mountains, the open sea and the volcano island, and then the garden, the marsh, the park, the canal town, the winter fair, the farm, the old tower, the ferry town, the painter's hut, the observatory and the old city. Between them they use twenty-three grounds, twenty-three paths, eight kinds of weather, six deep skies and all seven guide designs.

So we looked for kinds of place the map does not have at all, rather than more of the kinds it has. Every place on the map today is on land or on the water, at the same scale, in daylight or at night. The candidates below go under the sea, under the land, into the sky, down to a beetle's height, onto new shores and onto a far land, and each brings a light the map has not used: under water, a cave's lamplight, the high blue above the clouds, the blue hour before sunrise, the green light down in the grass.

Everything is invented. We borrowed no place, character or picture from a book, a film or a game, and we kept away from the pictures a place is often reduced to. The oasis is dunes, a spring, a well, palms, camels and a fox, with no people drawn as a costume; the waterhole and the raft market, which we did not pick, note the same care as a reason for their rank.

## How we ranked them

Each candidate was scored from one to five on six things, and ranked by the total, with ties broken by the first. How much a child of the years it serves would love going there. How far its look is from all twenty-three worlds: its light, its ground, its horizon and its landmark. How well the place suits what it would teach, and whether it gives a subject that is thin today a home. Whether it has a natural place on the map and a way of getting there that the map's story already holds. How little it needs that does not exist, counting drawings, a new light and new pieces of the map. And how little care it needs to stay respectful and invented rather than borrowed.

The cloud islands rank first because they are the only candidate whose way there is already on the map: the hot-air balloon that drifts across a child's country once a visit, as the map's rare sight, is the balloon that comes down for them and takes them up, and the sky above the map is a region nothing else uses. They also give weather a home, which the science lessons touch and no world holds, and weather is measurement and data a child can see. The reef and the caves tie second. Each is a place under the map that the map has never shown, each has a light nothing else has, and each teaches something its place makes obvious: a shoal of fish is an array, and a cave is where you hear an echo. The long grass is the one new world for the youngest children after the garden, and its idea, the same meadow at a beetle's height, needs no new land at all. The fossil cliffs are dinosaurs and deep time, which children of seven to ten ask about more than almost anything, on a coast the map already has. The oasis is the one far land in the six, the first desert on the map, and the place where water is measured because it is scarce.

The rest were close, and each has a reason in its row. The lamp islands and the bee orchard teach well but look too like places the map has: two lighthouses already carry the story, and the orchard's flowers and trees are the garden's, the meadow's, the farm's and the painter's hut's. The treetops are lovely and share big leaves and warm light with the volcano island and rope bridges with the cloud islands. The moon is space again after the night sky and the observatory, and its black ground breaks the rule that keeps a world's ground light. The kelp forest waits for the reef, since one world under the sea comes first. The waterhole and the raft market would need the most care, because each is easily drawn as one real country's picture of itself; we would keep the waterhole to the land and the animals and the market to rafts of fruit, and both also overlap the oasis and the canal town. The midnight sun, the geyser valley, the waterfall gorge and the clockmaker's workshop each sit close to the mountains, the volcano island, the canal town or the kitchen. The salt mirror has the most unusual horizon of all nineteen, a landscape standing on its reflection, but its idea is symmetry, which the painter's hut already holds, for twice the drawing. The whistling canes give pitch a place, which the park already does, and a grove of canes needs care not to borrow one culture's pictures of one.

## The six concept scenes

Each scene is one wide picture, 80 squares by 45 on 5 mm squared paper, of the world as a child would arrive in it: its light above the horizon, its ground and the way across it, the drawings standing in it at three depths, its guide at the gate saying its arrival line, and its moment, which the page can turn to. Like a world on the roll it has two materials. The water, sand, rock, cloud, grass and footprints are marks the scene draws itself, which are texture; everything else is a shelf drawing standing in it, which is a subject a question could use. Every scene is drawn with the shelf's seeded pen and the five markers, with the four new lights described below. On the Worlds tab a scene opens on its own, fitted to the whole screen as a canvas with its first day and its moment to turn between, and the page of worlds to come shows the six first as a contact sheet and then one at a time, each with a switch between the term's first day and its moment, the identity above written out beside it, the rare sight drawn small, since in the world it comes only once in a while, and the drawings added for it.

### The cloud islands

Deep blue overhead, paling to white where a sea of cloud begins below. Islands float over it at every height, each with grass on top and a point of layered rock underneath, roots hanging out of it and a waterfall pouring off its edge into the cloud. The balloon is moored on the nearer island with a windsock beside it, and the paper bird stands by it: "You are above the clouds now." A rope bridge is being built from that island to the highest one, where the weather station stands with its cups, its vane, its white box and its rain gauge. On the first day the bridge has four planks still to go, drawn in pencil; its moment is "The rope bridge reaches the top island." Gulls wheel, swifts go over, a rainbow stands on the cloud to the right, and the way across is a line of small clouds to step on, from the island the child stands on at the front. Once in a while geese pass below the islands in a V.

The path would carry weather and the seasons, reading and making weather charts, pictograms and line graphs of rainfall and temperature, measuring rain in millimetres and temperature in degrees, the compass points of the wind, and eighths of a sky in cloud, which is how a weather forecaster counts it, for years 2 to 4. The place suits them because everything in it is weather seen from above and measured on the spot, and the bridge's planks are counted until it is done.

### The coral reef

Under the water, looking across a reef. The surface is a bright band overhead with light breaking on it and the hull of the boat that let the bell down; shafts of sun slant through the water to the sand. The diving bell hangs on its chain at the end of a sandy channel edged with shells, its round windows lit, and the guide, the ink-blot dot, is beside it: "This is the reef. Look closely." The great coral head stands to the right: a round brain coral with its grooves, branching coral with pale tips, tube coral and sea fans like lace. A sea turtle swims overhead, a shoal of striped fish goes by in three rows of five, an octopus sits on a rock at the front, crabs and starfish are on the sand, and kelp sways at the edges. Its moment is the night once a year when the coral lets go of its eggs all together, drawn as pink snow rising from every part of the reef: "Pink snow rises from the coral." Once in a while a manta ray glides over.

The path would carry arrays and times tables, since a shoal is a grid of fish, symmetry in a starfish's five arms and a fish's two sides, depth below the surface as negative numbers, habitats and food chains, and a turtle's life cycle, for years 2 to 4.

### The crystal caves

A cave under the mountains, its roof dark lavender and hung with small stalactites, glow-worm lights all over it, bats hanging in a row and a curtain of glow-worm threads. A shaft of daylight comes down through a crack in the roof onto an underground lake, where a small boat is moored and a far cluster of crystals is reflected. The floor is pale flowstone lit by a lantern, with rimstone pools stepping down at the front, a great column where a stalactite and a stalagmite have met, and a line of chalk arrows leading to the crystal grotto, where the glow guide stands and shines its beam like a torch: "This is the cave. Hear the echo." On the first day the crystals are drawn unlit; the moment is "Every crystal lights up at once", and the grotto and the floor round it glow pink, blue and green. Once in a while at dusk the bats stream out past the lamp.

The path would carry sound, echoes and pitch (a long stalactite rings lower than a short one), light and shadow by lamplight, solid shapes (a crystal's faces, edges and corners), very slow growth and the very large numbers that go with it, since a stalactite grows about a finger's width in a hundred years, and comparing lengths, for years 3 to 5.

### The long grass

The meadow's grass at the height of a beetle. Blades of grass rise like trees into a warm morning sky, some bent over into arches, the low sun glowing through them from the left with rays between. Clover leaves stand like umbrellas, a spider waits in a web beaded with dew, and dew drops hang in the air like glass balls. The ground is soil with fallen leaves as big as rafts, pebbles like boulders and cushions of moss, and the way across is an ant trail with ants carrying crumbs and bits of leaf home. The firefly waits by two red toadstools: "You are as small as an ant." A dandelion clock stands as tall as a tree; its moment is "Puff. The dandelion seeds fly away", and its seeds are out over the grass. A ladybird sits on a stone. Once in a while a bumblebee as big as a bus flies over.

The path would carry measuring and scale (how many ants tall is a blade of grass), counting to twenty in spots, legs and seeds, taking away with the seeds that blew, minibeasts and their homes, life cycles, and symmetry in wings and webs, for kindergarten to year 2, the years the garden starts.

### The fossil cliffs

A tall cliff in bands of gold, peach, pink, blue and grey, tilted as rock layers are, with grass along its top. Set in the cliff is the skeleton of a long-necked sea reptile, from its small head along its neck of bones to its ribs, four paddles and tail; on the first day only a third of it is out of the rock and the rest is in pencil, and its moment is "The whole skeleton is out of the rock." The sea is to the right with a stack and an arch standing in it and a gull on the stack, and the ground is flat rock ledges at low tide with rock pools, ammonites set in the rock, crabs and a starfish. A trail of three-toed dinosaur footprints crosses the ledges to the foot of the cliff, where the snail waits: "These are the fossil cliffs. Look down." A sign by the pools shows the cliff's layers, youngest at the top and oldest at the bottom. Once in a while a seal looks up out of the waves.

The path would carry timelines and deep time, putting layers and events in order, place value into the millions of years, measuring and scale (how tall was the animal that made this footprint), sorting fossils into kinds, and spirals that grow by a rule, for years 3 to 5. It sits beside the old tower's history rather than on it: the tower holds the history of people, and the cliffs the history of the land.

### The oasis

The blue hour before sunrise on a far shore. The sky runs from deep blue with the last stars and a thin moon down through lilac and rose to gold, where the sun is half up behind the far dunes and its rays fan across the sky. The dunes stand one behind another, their crests sharp, the faces towards the sun gold and the slopes away from it lilac, and everything casts a long violet shadow. A pool lies among date palms and reeds, two camels rest on the sand, a fennec fox watches from a dune at the front, and camel tracks lead to a stone well with a bucket on its rope, where the hand waits: "This is the oasis. Here comes the sun." A sundial stands by the well with its shadow at seven in the morning. On the first day the sand is bare; the moment is "Rain in the night. The desert blooms", and the sand round the pool and over the dunes comes up in flowers. Once in a while a line of camels crosses the far dune.

The path would carry capacity (buckets from the well, litres and millilitres, how many buckets fill a trough), time from shadows at the sundial through the day, hot days and cold nights on a thermometer, reading the notes travellers leave at the well, and very small rainfalls on a chart, for years 2 to 4.

## Where they go on the map

The map grows, as [overworld.md](overworld.md) sets out, without anything already drawn being drawn again: every place is kept by its term's slot or its own site, and a land added later is drawn beside the old ones. The six extend it in five directions it has not grown in before, and we agreed each with the overworld work, which owns the geography.

- The cloud islands are up: in the sky over the northern ocean, north-east of the mountains, at about (6800, -9300) in map units, clear of the title, the compass and every world. They were to be reached by the hot-air balloon; as built they are reached by a path from the mountains, since a way through the air needs a rider on the map's own page, which this round could not change. On the map they are small islands drawn above the paper with their shadows on the sea below.
- The coral reef is down under the sea: on an atoll off the town's estuary, at (6000, 7000), inside the first sheet and clear of the southern islets. We moved it east from (-500, 7600) so its way leaves from the town it stands beside. The ship carries the bell out from the town's firth, and the bell goes down. On the map the atoll is a ring of sand round its lagoon with the reef showing through the water round it.
- The crystal caves are down under the land: inside the southern foot of the mountains, at about (4700, -2900), through a cave mouth near the rails that climb from the loch. The child walks in. On the map it is a cut into the hillside showing the cave and its crystals, the way a real map puts a detail in a box.
- The long grass is in: under the meadow's own grass. It was to be a magnifying lens lying in the meadow rather than a site. As built it is a site of its own in the grass south of the meadow, at (-9800, 2700), where it keeps the 2,400 units the map's test holds places apart, and its way leaves from the meadow.
- The fossil cliffs are a new shore on land already drawn: the tip of the home isle's long south-west peninsula, at about (-17000, 6600), along a path from the meadow, so they are on the map from the first year as the places a subject visits are.
- The oasis is across: on the dune coast at (6800, 11400), on a third sheet taped along the bottom of the first as the far shore is taped along its top, and reached by sea from the town's firth. It is the southern sheet's first world; the warm south is also where the treetops, the waterhole and the raft market would go if they are ever built.

What was built. Geography stays in `src/world/geography.ts`, the one place that says where things are. The six are in `SITES`; the atoll and the southern shore are in `LANDS`, drawn once a place on them is on the map, so neither appears before a world on it does; and the southern sheet, `SHEET_SOUTH`, joins the map's bounds when a place stands on it. On the worlds to come the six are drawn in pencil and ringed like the rest, and the marks the concept map drew for them (the atoll, the cut, the sky islands and the lens) are gone, since the places themselves are drawn now.

## The map itself, as art

We proposed these to the overworld work, which took one now and kept the rest for a later round. The seal is now one of the creatures a child spots from the paper plane, on the rocks at the tip of the home isle's peninsula, where a child's map reaches the sea from the second year. The sea turtle and the manta ray are on the shelf for the southern sea round the atoll, and wait for the atoll, since a child's map, and so the plane, only reaches sea near worlds the child has walked; flying fish for the southern sea are not drawn. Currents drawn as long dashed arrows in the open water, which the plane could ride, are a change to the wind in `fly.ts` and a good flight mechanic. A place under the sea or in the sky could be drawn as a small flap taped by one edge to the map, lifted to show what is under the water or above the clouds, which is the sketchbook's own way of showing a second level. The lens for the long grass, and rows in the map's key for a reef, a cave and a sky island, belong with the places once they are on the journal's map, and nothing was added to the key.

## How it is built

`src/world/next.ts` holds the candidates with their identities and scores, ranked in code. The six ranked first are declarations in `src/world/` like any world, `reef.ts`, `caves.ts`, `clouds.ts`, `grass.ts`, `fossils.ts` and `oasis.ts`, registered in `WORLDS`, each with a track site that holds real lessons by id under a word or two for what they are, reaches into those lessons, a chapter with its moment, secret and rare sight, wants, and a composition for the map. Their grounds and ways (reef, cavern, cloud tops, dunes, ledges and litter; a channel of shells, chalk arrows, clouds to step on, camel tracks, a trackway and an ant trail) and three weathers (the sunrise's rays, drips in the cave and dew in the grass) were ported from the concept painter into `src/world/paint.ts`, re-expressed as the world painter's washes, horizon marks and margin texture. `src/world/concept.ts` and the scene views on the worlds to come are deleted. `test/next.test.ts` holds the ranking and holds the six to being the six it ranked first; the world tests hold them to every rule a world has.

The four new lights are in `SKIES` in `src/world/check.ts`: reef, cave, high and dawn. The reef is the one light that is light at the top, so its name and pencil are dark; its stops were redrawn to stay light through the band a name can sit in, and the high and dawn stops to stay dark through it, so every name keeps 7 to 1 as check.ts measures it for any deep sky. The fossil cliffs and the long grass are lit with the palette's washes.

The six move as every world does, through their drawings' declared motion, and their rare sights play in the world's own player: a manta ray and a skein of geese drifting over, the bats streaming out of the cave, a line of camels along the far dune, a seal coming out of the waves, and a bumblebee as big as a bus.

## Candidates that became worlds

In the second round the owner asked for more worlds on the southern continent and for small islands across the seas, and we chose them so that every lesson outside maths would have a place of its own ([overworld.md](overworld.md), "The places further off, built"). Five of the nine built came from this list, each changed where the reason it was not picked asked for it. `next.ts` records each as `built`, and `test/next.test.ts` holds a candidate past the first six to being built only as the place it names.

- The treetops (the candidate `canopy`) became the treetops on the wet west end of the southern shore, for forces and motion. Its rope bridge is a landmark rather than the way across, so it does not repeat the cloud islands, and its ground is the crowns of the trees seen from a walkway rather than the volcano island's big leaves.
- The lamp islands became the lamp rocks at the north mouth of the strait, for the first coding lessons. Their lamps flash patterns a program can make, which is what separates them from the harbour's lighthouse.
- The geyser valley kept its name and moved to the east end of the southern shore, for heat, changes and acids.
- The salt mirror became the salt flats inland on the southern shore, for dissolving and separating, with the mirror kept as its moment. Symmetry stays with the painter's hut.
- The clockmaker's workshop became the clockwork island in the eastern sea, for the later coding lessons. It is outdoors on an island rather than up a stair in the town, which answers the reason it was not picked.

The other four places, the book island, the printing works, the post office and the windmill island, were not candidates. They were drawn for the reading, writing and physics lessons that had no place.

## What is not done

- The six hold only lessons the corpus already had. What each still wants is in its `needs`, and the plan for the strands they wait for is in [tracks.md](tracks.md).
- The cloud islands are reached by a path. A way through the air, with the balloon to ride, needs a rider in `engine/ui/map.ts` (`src/pages/map-life.ts` at the time), which this round could not change.
- The wants each lists are not drawn: a seahorse and a clownfish, an underground lake with a boat and a helmet with a lamp, a rain gauge and swallows, a caterpillar and a dew drop with the grass in it, a hammer and brush and a timeline on the cliff, a water bottle and hat and a lizard.
- The map proposals (sighting creatures, currents, flaps, key rows) are for a later round.
